# Chapter 3 — Extending the garage along the existing 1-ft side setback

**Document ID:** LAND-006  
**Revision:** 0  
**Date:** 2026-09-20  
**Status:** draft  
**Property:** 1370 Wilbur Avenue, Pacific Beach, San Diego, California 92109  
**Purpose:** Research record answering two questions: (1) does the City of San Diego have a code route that lets the existing garage be extended along its existing approximately 1-ft east side setback instead of the 4-ft RS-1-7 setback, and (2) how can permit history, approved plans and prior decisions be looked up to find precedent. Not a permit determination or a survey. Supplements LAND-005.

## 3.1 Answer in brief

There are two written code routes that allow a structure to sit inside the 4-ft side setback, and neither depends on finding a precedent decision.

1. **By right, §131.0461(a)(12):** a detached garage or non-habitable accessory building may encroach into a required side or rear yard with no stated minimum distance, provided the lot is 10,000 sq ft or less, the building is no more than 15 ft high within the setback, is not on a common wall with the house, runs no more than 30 ft within any one setback, and has no more than 525 sq ft of gross floor area. The existing garage (about 20.8 × 20.8 ft, 431 sq ft, 13.2-ft ridge) already meets every condition. Under this route roughly **9 ft more length** and **94 sq ft more floor area** could be added along the east line with an ordinary building permit.

2. **Discretionary, §127.0106(b):** a previously conforming structure may be expanded "conform[ing] to the setback observed by the existing structure" with a Neighborhood Development Permit (Process Two, staff decision, mailed notice, no hearing). Nine criteria apply; the ones that bind here are first-story-only, no taller than the existing structure within the setback, and total length in the yard no more than 50 percent of the property line (about 61 ft, so about **40 ft more length**). Flat fee is $14,838.42 plus about $1,277 administrative fees, or an $8,000 deposit account.

The critical limitation for this project is the same in both routes: **nothing above one story may sit inside the setback.** Route 1 says "any development attached to the accessory building above one story shall comply with the setback"; route 2 says additions are "limited to additions at the first story level" and may not "exceed the height of the existing structure within the setback." A steel frame standing in the 1-ft strip is therefore possible only as a single-story element no taller than the existing garage, with the upper storage level held back to the 4-ft line. The 525 sq ft cap in route 1 also counts every floor of the building, so a building with an upper storage floor over the full footprint will not fit route 1 and falls to route 2, which then requires the frame in the setback to stay at or below the existing 13.2-ft ridge.

A design that uses the 1-ft strip only for first-story steel columns and a roof no higher than the existing garage roof is consistent with both routes. Which route applies depends on whether the final building stays under 525 sq ft and 30 ft of setback length.

## 3.3 Route 1: accessory-building encroachment, §131.0461(a)(12)

Text (SDMC Ch. 13 Art. 1 Div. 4, 7-2026 edition) [S4]:

> (12) Garages or non-habitable accessory buildings may encroach into a required side or rear yard as follows:  
> (A) The lot size shall not exceed 10,000 square feet;  
> (B) The encroaching accessory building shall be limited to a maximum structure height of 15 feet within the setback;  
> (C) The encroaching accessory building shall not share a common wall with the primary dwelling unit, but may be attached via a non-structural design element. Any development attached to the accessory building above one story shall comply with the setback;  
> (D) The accessory building shall not exceed a maximum length of 30 feet within any given setback; and  
> (E) An encroaching accessory building shall not exceed 525 square feet in gross floor area.

§131.0448(c) confirms: "Non-habitable accessory buildings or garages may encroach into required yards subject to the requirements in Section 131.0461." Table 131-04D lists §131.0461(a) as applying in RS-1-7.

Checks against this project:

| Condition | Existing garage | Extended garage |
|---|---|---|
| Lot ≤ 10,000 sq ft | 5,994 sq ft, passes | passes |
| ≤ 15 ft structure height within setback | 13.2 ft ridge, passes | any new roof over the 1-ft strip must stay ≤ 15 ft measured per §113.0270 |
| No common wall with dwelling | detached, passes | passes if it stays detached |
| ≤ 30 ft length within the east setback | 20.8 ft, passes | at most about 9 ft of added length |
| ≤ 525 sq ft gross floor area | 431 sq ft, passes | at most about 94 sq ft added, counting all floors |
| Nothing above one story in the setback | single story, passes | upper level must be at the 4-ft line |

Points to note:

- No minimum distance is stated, so the encroachment may run to the property line. Staff practice supports this: in 2020 DSD ministerially approved a detached garage on the rear lot line at 416 Nautilus St, La Jolla (RM-1-1, coastal), citing "the consistent interpretation of SDMC 131.0448(c), 131.0461(a)(12), and 141.0302(a)(6)." That quotation comes from a neighbor's objection letter [S5], not from the permit itself.
- Gross floor area under §113.0234(a)(1) "includes all existing and proposed floors within the horizontal area delineated by the exterior surface of the surrounding exterior walls." A storage loft over the full footprint would roughly double the count and exceed 525 sq ft. There is no attic or storage exclusion in §113.0234(d).
- The preamble to §131.0461(a) bars these encroachments "in the required yards within view corridors that are designated by land use plans in the Coastal Overlay Zone." Wilbur Avenue is not between the shoreline and the first public roadway, and no designated view corridor is known at this parcel; confirm in the Pacific Beach Community Plan and Local Coastal Program.
- §131.0461 was last amended by O-21416 (effective 2-26-2022) and carries no editor's note deferring it in the Coastal Overlay Zone, unlike §131.0448 and §141.0302, so (a)(12) is treated here as effective in the coastal zone. Confirm at the counter.
- Roof projections under §131.0461(a)(1)(B) may not be closer than 2 ft 6 in to the property line. With a 1-ft wall there can be no eave beyond the wall face on the east side; the building code limit in §3.6 is tighter still.

If the building is instead treated as previously conforming rather than conforming under (a)(12), route 2 applies; the two are not mutually exclusive, and DSD will apply whichever the final drawings satisfy.

## 3.4 Route 2: previously conforming expansion, §127.0106(b)

Text (SDMC Ch. 12 Art. 7 Div. 1, 3-2026 edition) [S6]:

> (b) Proposed expansion or enlargement of a previously conforming structural envelope within a setback ... requires a Neighborhood Development Permit decided in accordance with Process Two, which shall only be granted if the proposed expansion or enlargement meets all of the following criteria:  
> (1) Conforms to the setback observed by the existing structure;  
> (2) Complies with the floor area ratio and maximum structure height of the underlying base zone;  
> (3) Does not encroach into a front yard or extend outside of the developable area of the underlying base zone to within 10 feet of the front yard setback line, unless the proposed expansion or enlargement would reduce the non-conformity of existing development;  
> (4) Does not encroach more than 15 feet into any required side or rear yard;  
> (5) Does not result in a total structure length within the required yard that is greater than 50 percent of the length of the adjacent property line;  
> (6) Does not create any new habitable space within 3 feet of the property line;  
> (7) Is limited to additions at the first story level (as measured in accordance with Section 113.0261) and does not exceed the height of the existing structure within the setback;  
> (8) Does not result in more dwelling units than the underlying base zone allows; and  
> (9) Does not propose development on a premises that contains or abuts a coastal beach or coastal bluff edge.

Checks:

|---|---|
| (1) same setback as existing | extension holds the 1-ft line, passes |
| (2) FAR and 24/30-ft height of RS-1-7 | to be checked with the house included; not expected to bind |
| (3) front yard | garage is at the rear, passes |
| (4) ≤ 15 ft encroachment | 3 ft, passes |

| (8) dwelling units | none added, passes |
| (9) beach or bluff premises | not applicable, passes |

Table 127-01A footnote 1 is the penalty for missing a criterion: "Development that does not meet the criteria for a Process One or Process Two approval shall comply with all current regulations and the previously conforming aspect of the premises shall terminate." An application under this route that fails criterion (7) is not just denied; it forfeits the 1-ft line.

Precondition: the garage must actually be previously conforming, meaning it "complied with all applicable state and local laws when it was first built" (§113.0103, §127.0102(a)). An unpermitted 1-ft setback has no §127.0106 rights. This is why the permit-history lookup in §3.7 matters.

Process Two (§112.0503): a designated staff person decides without a public hearing after mailing a Notice of Future Decision; the decision is made no sooner than 11 business days after mailing; appeal is to the Planning Commission (§112.0504). Fees from Information Bulletin 503 (September 2026): Neighborhood Development Permit flat fee $14,838.42, plus Discretionary Project Closeout $1,264.48 and mapping $12.16; the deposit-account alternative is $8,000 initial with a $4,000 minimum balance; an environmental exemption determination is $1,412.54 [S7]. Expect a total near $17,500 before consultants.

The City's "Previously Conforming Uses Fact Sheet" [S8] still describes the pre-2015 rule ("a reduced setback of up to 20% of the new required setback"). That language was replaced by O-20482, effective 6-4-2015, with the criteria above. Do not rely on the fact sheet.

## 3.5 Coastal Development Permit

The parcel is in the Coastal Overlay Zone, non-appealable jurisdiction. §126.0704(a) exempts "improvements to existing structures" unless, among other triggers, the work removes 50 percent or more of the exterior walls (a)(5), or the 10-percent floor-area and height triggers in (a)(2) apply, which are limited to structures "between the sea and first public roadway paralleling the sea or within 300 feet of the inland extent of any beach" [S9]. 14 CCR §13250 treats garages and storage sheds as part of a single-family residence for this exemption. An extension that keeps more than half of the garage's exterior walls should qualify for a counter exemption determination; removing the old roof does not by itself count as wall removal, but the plan to rebuild the slab, add piers and reframe needs a §127.0111 wall-retention calculation (Information Bulletin 402). If a CDP is required, the Process Two CDP fee for a single dwelling unit is $30,914.15 [S7], and §127.0104(b) then routes even maintenance of the previously conforming envelope through a Neighborhood Development Permit.

## 3.6 Building code at a 1-ft fire separation distance

California Residential Code Table R302.1(1), unmodified by the City's 2025 amendments in SDMC §149.0105 [S10]:

| Element | Under 2 ft | 2 ft to under 3 ft | 3 ft to under 5 ft | 5 ft and over |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wall | 1-hour, rated from both sides | 1-hour | 1-hour | not rated |
| Projections | not allowed | 1-hour underside, heavy timber, or fire-retardant wood | same | not rated |
| Openings | not allowed | not allowed | 25% of wall area | unlimited |

Exception 4: "Detached garages accessory to a dwelling located within 2 feet of a lot line are permitted to have roof eave projections not exceeding 4 inches." So the east wall of anything in the strip is a listed one-hour assembly rated from both sides, has no openings, and has at most a 4-inch eave. Exposed steel is not itself a rated assembly; the frame in the strip needs a rated enclosure or rated protection.

Filter GIS_ADDRESS for "1370 Wilbur Av" or filter APPROVAL_TYPE and scope text for precedent hunting. 6. **Approved plans (paper or microfilm, about 1955 on).** Information Bulletin 110 and https://www.sandiego.gov/development-services/records/architectural-building-plans-request . Anyone may view plans for any address by appointment at 7650 Mission Valley Road, but may not copy, trace or photograph. Copies require a Plan Duplication Application at the counter: the owner shows ID, the City mails the architect or engineer of record for consent under Health and Safety Code §§19850 to 19853, turnaround up to six weeks, $0.25 per page. Pre-1955 records are at the County Assessor.
7. **Public records requests.** https://sandiego.nextrequest.com/ for inspection histories, correspondence and discretionary permit files. Not for stamped plan sets.
8. **Decisions.** Hearing Officer reports (Process Three): https://www.sandiego.gov/development-services/public-hearings-meetings-notices/hearing-officer/reports , with agendas and minutes on sibling pages. Planning Commission reports (Process Four and appeals): https://www.sandiego.gov/planning-commission/documents/pcreports . Process Two decisions such as a §127.0106(b) NDP are staff decisions and are published only as a Notice of Future Decision in the City Clerk's Land Use and Development notices ( https://www.sandiego.gov/city-clerk/officialdocs/public-notices ); the decision itself is in the Accela record or by records request. There is no searchable decision database. Google full-text searches restricted to sandiego.gov with phrases such as "Neighborhood Development Permit" "side setback" "Pacific Beach" are the practical tool.

## 3.9 What this changes for the design

1. Steel columns and any roof in the 1-ft strip are limited to one story and to the existing garage height within the setback under either route. LAND-005's question about a 15-ft roof slope over the strip is answered for route 1 (15 ft is the cap) and route 2 (existing height, about 13.2 ft, is the cap).
2. The upper storage level must stop at the 4-ft line (or 3.9 ft if the survey shows the lot under 50 ft wide).
3. If the finished building, all floors counted, exceeds 525 sq ft or 30 ft of length in the east setback, the by-right route is gone and a Neighborhood Development Permit near $17,500 in City fees is required, and the garage's previously conforming status must be proven with permit records.
4. The east wall in the strip is a one-hour assembly rated from both sides with no openings and at most a 4-inch eave.
5. Keep more than half of the existing exterior walls, by the §127.0111 calculation, to stay inside the Coastal Development Permit exemption and to keep previously conforming status.
