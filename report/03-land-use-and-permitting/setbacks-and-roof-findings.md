## 3.1 Findings and present conclusion

There is a potential route to retain the existing garage close to the side boundary while constructing an independently supported, inset storage level. The legal basis for retaining the lower building and the allowable shape of its replacement roof remain unresolved. The critical roof question is whether a new slope over the three-foot-wide strip of retained garage may rise to 15 feet within the side setback under the accessory-building exception.

The research found a height restriction in that exception, not a standalone maximum roof-pitch number. This does not establish that the exception applies to this project. A steep roof reaching 15 feet is a conditional study option, not an adopted or approved envelope.

## 3.2 Owner-confirmed intent and reported conditions

The owner clarified during the September 15 discussion:

- The upstairs is **storage space**, not a proposed ADU or sleeping accommodation.
- All existing garage walls remain exterior. Exterior posts stand over open walkways; no new ground-floor perimeter walls enclose the old walls.
- New steel framing and independent concrete piers are intended to carry the new roof and upper floor. The old roof will be removed.
- The existing slab is reportedly about four inches thick, badly cracked and settling, with little apparent ground preparation. Existing footings are unknown.
- Much of the existing construction reportedly uses reclaimed lumber, including tongue-and-groove boards; possible former barracks provenance is unverified.
- The existing side clearance is approximately one foot; the alley clearance is approximately 6 feet 10 inches. These are owner estimates, not surveyed dimensions.

These statements establish design intent and investigation needs. They do not establish legal construction history, structural capacity, occupancy classification, or foundation adequacy. No new load-bearing capacity is assigned to the slab or existing walls in this report.

## 3.3 Evidence from working drawings

The existing OBJ and site model were reviewed alongside exterior and exposed-frame renderings. The renderings support the owner's explanation: the lower walkways are open and the existing garage walls remain exterior. The earlier conversational suggestion that exterior posts necessarily enclose the old walls is withdrawn.

The existing OBJ has a nominal 249.5 by 249 inch footprint, approximately 431 square feet. Its floor is a datum surface, not a modeled foundation. A later construction study instead provisionally uses 286.25 inches north–south, approximately 496 square feet at the same width. That conflict is unresolved; neither version is adopted here. The site-model fence positions are estimates.

One support study places the east beam centerline at X = 224.25 inches against an existing east wall at X = 249.5 inches. If the wall is exactly 12 inches inside the boundary, this places the beam centerline 37.25 inches from it. The result flags a possible conflict with a 48-inch wall setback; it does not determine the finished wall position. Member thickness, cladding, surveyed boundaries and the selected revision must be reconciled.

## 3.5 Alley conclusion and its limits

A nominal 20-foot alley and the standard rear-yard calculation produce a five-foot on-site setback; 20 + 5 = 25 feet also exceeds the parking-access distance. That explains why the owner's approximately 6-foot-10-inch clearance appears compatible with the standard calculation.

The earlier conversational five-foot answer was conditional, not a parcel-specific determination. Accessory exceptions, overlays, existing approvals, and the recorded alley boundary still need coordination. Do not move the north wall or loading ledge on the strength of the GIS estimate.

## 3.6 Roof over the retained side strip

Assume, for this geometric example only, that the old wall is one foot from the boundary and the new upper wall is four feet from it. The retained first-floor interior extends three feet beyond the upper wall and needs weather protection. The owner proposes a continuous sloped roof over that strip, preferably steep, with no upper storage floor extending into it.

Using a low reference elevation of 8 feet 2.5 inches from the working model, and a 36-inch horizontal run:

| Pitch, rise:run | Rise over 36 inches | High reference elevation |
|---|---:|---:|
| 4:12 | 12 inches | 9 feet 2.5 inches |
| 12:12 | 36 inches | 11 feet 2.5 inches |
| 24:12 | 72 inches | 14 feet 2.5 inches |
| 27.167:12 | 81.5 inches | 15 feet |

Calculation: (180 − 98.5) / 36 = 2.2639 vertical/horizontal, approximately 66.17 degrees. This is geometry, not a permitted maximum pitch. The wall-top elevation is not automatically the finished roof-surface elevation. Roof buildup, drainage edges, actual run, grade datum and statutory height measurement must be incorporated before using this table in a drawing.

A dimensioned cross-section must establish where the roof crosses the side setback and whether the City regards the encroaching portion as roofing of the retained first story or development above one story. A void without flooring does not itself settle that classification. Do not assume that the roof can reach the full upper-story height within the setback, or that keeping its low edge unchanged authorizes raising the rest of it.

## 3.7 Existing-building and coastal review

SDMC §127.0104 addresses maintenance, repair, alteration and replacement of previously conforming structural envelopes. Section 127.0106(a)(1) permits qualifying additions where new work meets current setbacks, height and floor-area requirements without increasing nonconformity. The alternative expansion route in §127.0106(b) includes first-story and existing-height limitations within the setback. That route does not independently supply a new 15-foot allowance [S4].

There is no universal rule that keeping half the walls guarantees retention of all rights. The provisions differ for use, structural envelope, coastal permit triggers and beach/bluff circumstances. Determine the property's overlays and applicable approval route before specifying demolition.

City Bulletin 402 explains exterior-wall demolition calculations, including treatment of walls enclosed by new construction [S5]. The owner's open-post scheme does not propose that enclosure. Nevertheless, actual wall replacement, reframing, altered openings and any changed exterior status need a demolition/retention schedule. Bulletin 402 does not itself decide the roof question.

## 3.8 Building/fire and foundation coordination

The intended use is storage. Do not apply ADU setback entitlements, dwelling sprinkler exceptions, or dwelling-over-garage ceiling provisions as though a residence were proposed. The responsible designer must establish the applicable California code scope and occupancy, storage loads, access/egress, guards, and fire requirements. City guidance identifies the 2025 code cycle and local amendments [S6].

Earlier residential-code screening identified rated exterior walls near boundaries, restricted openings and protected penetrations as likely issues. It is not a completed analysis for this storage configuration. LIFE-001 must determine applicable provisions, existing-work upgrade scope, roof/soffit protection, openings and any protection required for supporting steel.

Steel siding and framing are not proof of an hourly fire rating. Required ratings must be established by approved assemblies and protection details; arbitrary insulation behind metal panels is insufficient [S7].

Independent drilled concrete piers can provide a separate load path in principle. Their dimensions, reinforcement, soil resistance, uplift/lateral behavior and column connections remain engineering tasks. Old-wall stability after roof removal and excavation next to uncertain footings require assessment even if the new structure puts no gravity load on the old garage.

## 3.9 Questions for written City response

No agency interpretation has been requested or received as part of this research. Submit a surveyed plan and dimensioned existing/proposed section with these questions:

1. **Roof classification:** May the retained garage strip between the approximately one-foot existing wall clearance and four-foot upper-wall setback receive a new slope rising to 15 feet, without any second-floor area in that strip? Which provision governs?
2. **Height and geometry:** Where and how must height be measured along that slope? How does the restriction on development above one story apply? What roof geometry would be acceptable if the proposed slope is not?
3. **Area limit:** How does the 525-square-foot accessory-building condition apply to the retained garage plus new storage floor? Which areas count, and is a previously conforming route available if that condition is exceeded?
4. **Retained building:** What permit records establish the existing footprint's legal status, and what repair, slab replacement, wall strengthening or independent-foundation work may occur without losing the relevant allowance?
5. **Open supports:** How are the exterior columns, overhead framing, upper walkways and roof edges measured for setbacks and coverage when the lower walkways remain unenclosed?
6. **Coastal/other controls:** What overlays, coastal permit requirements, height restrictions, easements or prior approvals apply to this parcel and scope?
7. **Storage classification:** Which building/fire code provisions govern the two-level garage/storage arrangement and retained exterior wall at approximately one foot?

Record responses against OI-013 through OI-017. Do not label these interpretations verified until the response and its drawing basis are retained in Chapter 3.
