# Patio awning near the property line — San Diego code research

2026-09-29. Working research note, not a permit determination. Parcel facts from LAND-006 (report/03-land-use-and-permitting/setback-extension-research.md): 1370 Wilbur Ave, RS-1-7, Coastal Overlay N-APP-2 (non-appealable), lot ≈ 49 × 122 ft.

## Answer in brief

With posts on the west fence line the awning is inside the required side yard, and the City's rule for this kind of structure is **5 ft from any property line**. The distances that matter:

| Distance from the property line | What the code says |
|---|---|
| 0 to under 2 ft | CRC Table R302.1(1): roof projections **not allowed**. SDMC: no residential projection or patio rule allows it. |
| 2 ft to under 2 ft 6 in | CRC allows a projection with 1-hr underside, heavy timber or FRT wood; SDMC still bars it (every projection rule stops at 2′-6″). |
| 2 ft 6 in to under 5 ft | SDMC allows only building-attached projections: eaves (a)(1), and openly supported projections/trellises (a)(2) that are ≥ 50 % open in plan, up to 6 ft or half the yard deep. A solid fabric sheet on posts is neither. CRC: 1-hr underside or heavy timber/FRT for projections under 5 ft. |
| 5 ft and more | SDMC §131.0461(a)(8): patio structures may sit in the side or rear yard. |
| Outside the required yard (side 4 ft, or 8 % of width ≈ 3.9 ft; rear 13 ft, alley credit may leave 5 ft) | No encroachment rule needed. Permit-exempt if ≤ 300 sf, ≤ 12 ft high and not in any yard (§129.0203(a)(12)). |

So a fabric roof on the P2–P3–P4 fence posts does not fit any by-right rule. The posts stop being "fence" once they carry the awning: SDMC defines a fence as a barrier that "supports no load other than its own weight."

## Sources read

- **SDMC §131.0461(a)** (Ch 13 Art 1 Div 4, 9-2026 edition), permitted projections and encroachments in RS zones:
  - (1) eaves: ≤ 6 ft or 50 % of yard into it, **not closer than 2′-6″** to the line, 6′-8″ clearance.
  - (2) openly supported projections incl. trellises: ≤ eave height, **≥ 50 % open in plan**, ≤ 6 ft or 50 % of yard, **not closer than 2′-6″**, 6′-8″ clear. (Diagram 131-04T shows them projecting from a building wall.)
  - (8) **patio structures** in side or rear yard: **not closer than 5 ft to any property line**, open on at least three sides except columns ≤ 18 × 18 in, columns ≥ 8 ft on centre, roof no higher than the house eave or second-floor sill.
  - (12) non-habitable accessory buildings (the garage route in LAND-006): no stated minimum, ≤ 15 ft high, ≤ 30 ft long in a setback, ≤ 525 sf GFA.
- **SDMC §113.0103 definitions:** "Awning" is supported entirely from a building's exterior wall, so a post-supported sheet is not an awning under the Code. "Fence" supports no load but its own weight. "Structure" includes a post or shelter.
- **SDMC §129.0203(a)(12):** patio covers are exempt from a building permit if ≤ 300 sf projected roof, not in any yard, ≤ 12 ft high, and not in the appealable Coastal area or a Planned Development. (a)(2): fences ≤ 7 ft need no permit. (a)(10): wall-hung window awnings ≤ 54 in projection.
- **IB 206 Patio Covers** (rev. 3-2026): one-story, ≤ 12 ft, open on two or more sides; fire separation per CRC "based on the distance … from a real or assumed lot line"; Class A roof covering assembly; lists the Coastal Zone as needing a permit, which is stricter than the SDMC text.
- **SDMC §142.0310(d):** fences in side and rear yards up to 9 ft; anything above 6 ft must be open fence.
- **SDMC §126.0704(a):** improvements to existing structures are exempt from a Coastal Development Permit unless on a beach or bluff, adding ≥ 10 % floor area, intensifying use, etc. A patio cover here should be exempt; confirm at intake.
- **CRC 2025 Table R302.1(1):** projections under 2 ft fire separation distance are not allowed; 2 to under 5 ft need 1-hr underside, heavy timber or fire-retardant-treated wood.

## What it means for this design

- Fabric plan area is about 162 sf (under the 300 sf exemption size), but the exemption is lost because the awning is in a yard.
- **Posts P2, P3 and P4 are on the line.** To comply by right, the awning's edge and any posts carrying it would have to stay 5 ft off the west line, and 5 ft off the rear line if P1–P2 is near it. About 64 sf of the current 162 sf lies within 5 ft of the west line.
- The pillar end (T1, T2) is part of the garage, which already sits in the east setback under (a)(12). Whether the City would treat a fabric roof attached to it as part of that accessory building is an open question. It would have to count toward the 30 ft and 525 sf limits and stay one story; an open-sided cover is not obviously a "building."
- Water: about 40 % of the roof sheds off the P2–P3 edge on the fence. Nothing found in the zoning code on this, but dumping roof water onto a neighbour is a civil-nuisance risk; a gutter along that edge is advisable in any scheme.
- Fabric: IB 206 asks for a Class A roof covering; fabric may need a listed flame-resistant (e.g. NFPA 701) material, which plan check would decide.
